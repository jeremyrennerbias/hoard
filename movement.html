General/unspecific liberatory subcultures

Unvalid

A person who (aggressively) rejects the concept of "validity" as it is used in online communities. You do not need anyone's approval to identify how you want, because why would you?

[Source]

Villainpunk/Vilpunk

A punk subculture dedicated to the reclamation of demonization, a rejection of the social norm of goodness, taking pride in identities that carry a stigma.

[Source]

Inclusivity Punk/Incluspunk

A name for the subculture & community built around radical inclusionism. It shows support of anyone & everyone so long as no cultures, communities, and/or individuals are being harmed and/or disrespected. This subculture supports the ideas of cringe being dead, that nobody is free until we're all free, and that we should build one another up rather than contribute to infighting; this is inclusive of any & every label -- which are inherently unaligned -- and encourages showing nuance, compassion and open-mindedness towards others' experiences as a whole.

[Source]

Inclusionist

An inclusionist, or inclus, is any individual who is inclusive to all identities within the LGBTQIA+ community. This includes xenogenders, neopronouns, a-specs, m-specs, and other non-harmful and good-faith identity. Inclusionists work together to make the community a safer place to live and be in.

[Source]

Intersectionality

An analytical framework for understanding how groups' and individuals' social and political identities result in unique combinations of discrimination and privilege. Examples of these intersecting and overlapping factors include gender, caste, sex, race, ethnicity, class, sexuality, religion, disability, physical appearance, and age. These factors can lead to both empowerment and oppression.

[Flag source][Definition source]

Amorality

A disregard for morality as a valid framework of assessing how harmful something is. It believes that there are many groups for which moral codes can be oppressive (such as the young, the elderly, the mentally ill, and disabled people) due to the immaterial nature of morality.

[Source]

Choicepunk

A subculture for those who are able to choose their identity/parts of their identity that they're told they can't choose. For people with chosen genders, chosen kin, chosen plurality, everything that they're told they can't choose but yet, they do.

[Source]

Radical Bodily Autonomy

The belief that every person has the right to make decisions about their own body, identity, recovery, relationships, and self-expression, provided they have access to accurate, comprehensive information about the potential risks, consequences, and alternatives.

[Source]

Constructqueer/
Constructpunk

The emphasis on how several facets of identity, such as gender and species, are actually social constructs, in the face of a society who widely believe them to be concrete facts. The belief that identity is wholly malleable.

[Source]

Subcultures pertaining to queer liberation

Amogai

A term describing one who loves the MOGAI community, coining terms, making flags, and being proud of who they are. Amogai people advocate for support and kindness no matter what.

[Source]

Contrapunk

A movement which opposes the idea that labels and personal identity can be ''contradictory,'' and the idea that people's identities should be ''befitting'' them.

[Source]

Contraqueer

A movement of people with ''contradictory'' identities, who embrace and are proud of their ''contradiction.''

[Source]


Queerfuck

A movement that directly opposed to exclusionist and binary rhetoric in the community. One who's queerfuck may have ''contradictory'' labels, and take pride in confusing and pissing off exclusionists. Queerfuck can be used as an orientation, or its own stand-alone term, similar to Genderfuck. Queerfucks use whatever words they want to describe their orientation, and the relationship between their gender & orientation.

[Source]

Subcultures pertaining to transgender+ liberation

Binary Abolitionism

Binary abolitionism, or radical binary abolitionism, seeks to destroy binaries between genders and instead have them exist like washcloths and squirrels: simultaneously and with no binary between them. Male and female are not opposites and there is no difference between them and any other gender one may be.

[Source]

Genderpunk

A person who does not subscribe wholly to any prescribed gender distinctions for themself but finds the most empowerment in emphasizing the uniqueness of their own gender expression, experience, and identity, identifying with other individuals on a shared-experience basis rather than a shared-label basis.

[Source]

Postgenderism

A social, political and cultural movement which arose from the eroding of the cultural, psychological, and social role of gender, and an argument for why the erosion of binary gender will be liberatory.

[Source]


Transfeminism

A branch of feminism focused on transgender women and informed by transgender studies. Transfeminism focuses on the effects of transmisogyny and patriarchy on trans women.

[Flag source] [Definition source]

Transhazard

An identity based in unapologetic transness and opposition to cisnormative gender; a rejection of societal gender norms as a universal concept and an embracing of all those outcast by it. To be transhazard is to be recklessly, revolutionarily proud of oneself, one's transness, and one's community, and to uplift and support others regardless of conventionality.

[Source]

Trenderpunk

A subculture surrounded around trans self-love, self-acceptance, punk aesthetics, social justice, and identifying with the term ''trender''/''transtrender''.

[Source]

Lovequeer

Platopunk

An ideology and identity that is centred around challenging platonormativity, the way we define friendship and platonic relationships, as well as recognising the aplatonic spectrum and different forms of nontraditional friendship.

Relationship Anarchy

Subcultures pertaining to neurodivergent liberation

Psychpunk

A movement which advocates for the rights of neurodiverse, mentally ill, and mentally disabled people to have more autonomy over their lives and treatment. It pushes back against the current establishment of psychiatry that takes away autonomy, facilitates abuse, and is built on systemic racism, sexism, ableism, and more. Psychpunk advocates for the acknowledgement that diagnostic criteria can sometimes be reductive, restrictive, or outright harmful, and that they cannot accurately describe the entire range of mental health experiences.

[Source]

Mad Pride

A movement that celebrates madness as a cultural identity, challenges psychiatric oppression, and fosters community among those labeled as 'mentally ill.'

[Flag source][Definition source]

Anti-Psychiatry

A movement which considers psychiatry a coercive instrument of oppression due to an unequal power relationship between doctor, therapist, and patient or client, and a highly subjective diagnostic process. It believes that involuntary commitment, also known as sectioning, is an unjust violation of human agency.

[Flag source][Definition source]


Choice-Recovery

A stance on recovery, mostly in reference to disorders, paraphilias, self destructive habits or lifestyles, and drug/alcohol use. Choice-Recovery refers to the idea that nobody should be forced or coerced into any sort of recovery, especially if they are not causing harm to others.

[Source]

Dangerous Madpunk

A movement that's focused on completely destigmatizing mental illnesses that people tend to see as ''dangerous'', including NPD, ASPD, age- and species-related paraphilias, schizophrenia, etc.

[Source]

Parapunk

A belief in the destigmatization of paraphilias, and the liberation and unity of paraphiles.

[Source]

Pluralpunk/Plurpunk

A movement that focuses on embracing one's plurality and all of its intricacies in a radically accepting way as well as challenging the accepted ''norms'' of plurality within their own system and identity.

[Source]

Attentionpunk

A label intended to reclaim ways in which people are maligned for wanting attention, assumed to be doing something "for attention," being seen as faking being queer for attention, and other similar experiences. One way attentionpunk can be seen is a way to say "So what if I'm doing it for attention? Am I not allowed to want attention?"

[Source]

Subcultures pertaining to alterhuman liberation

Isofact

The belief that factfolk are valid, deserve to be taken seriously, and should not have their identities belittled.

[Source]

Isofic

The belief that fictionfolk are valid, deserve to be taken seriously, and should not have their identities belittled.

[Source]

Voidpunk

A subculture amongst dehumanized groups (including queer individuals, notably aspec and non-binary) where one rejects their humanity and embraces a non-human identity. This is often done to cope with or combat the dehumanization one is subjected to from society, however the reasons can vary from member to member.

[Source]


Otherpunk

The belief in full acceptance of alterhuman identities, supporting differences between one another, inclusion of one another, and defying strict lines or gatekeeping between identities.

[Source]

Dimensionpunk/Realitypunk

An ideology focused on embracing infinite/multiple dimensions. It is meant to empower gateway, dreamway, dimension hoppers, dimension voyagers, system hoppers, kin who believe in infinite dimensions, MaDD people who create daydream dimensions, and anyone whose identity, thoughts, being, etc are affected by multiple dimensions.

[Source]
